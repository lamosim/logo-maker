# Trends

Read this when: scoring `timeless` (criterion 6), writing the category audit, choosing territory styles, and whenever the owner asks for something fashionable.

The 2025 failure mode was "mid", not "bad": "the enemy of a good brand is no longer a bad brand, but one that is just okay" (Mother Design, Creative Boom, 7 Jan 2026). Aim for one ownable idea executed with restraint [05 §0, §1.1]. 2026's trend reports describe behaviour, not style: identities that "flex, pulse, reveal layers, adapt" (LogoLounge 2026) [05 §0]. Every item below carries its source and the source's date; dated as of October 2026 [05 §1.2, §1.4].

## Use

Durable, low risk [05 §1.4]:

- A flexible system with one fixed anchor mark. Designers now "build them frameworks" (Koto for MassiveMusic), but every flexible system needs a fixed anchor (LogoLounge 2026, June 2026; Creative Boom, 18 Dec 2025).
- Motion-ready construction: rotation, reveal, phase. "Animation has become a primary identifier, not just a decorative layer"; design the still frame to survive without the motion (LogoLounge 2025, June 2025).
- A custom wordmark with one owned typographic quirk, such as one italic letter in a roman word ("Slant Break", LogoLounge 2026). Custom wordmarks are durable; over-fused "Typemelts" are "a quick visual flex" (LogoLounge 2025).
- Mascots and characters where warmth is the strategy: "we respond to humanity" (LogoLounge 2025). The Cracker Barrel reversal (Aug 2025) proved what a character is worth.
- Square, app-tile-aware geometry: designers are "cramming what once lived on billboards into the palm of your hand", so marks need "razor-sharp icons that read instantly" (LogoLounge 2025). Small-screen-first construction is a permanent requirement.
- Electric green or YInMn blue as a secondary palette move: "Green is having a moment (actually, more like a takeover)" (LogoLounge 2026); YInMn persists from 2025.
- Micro-detail "technical" graphics for expertise brands: "the aesthetics of technical information" (It's Nice That, 12 Jan 2026).
- Character after "blanding": Monotype's 2024 report named "Quirk" and "Return of the Serif" as "a bold counterargument to play-it-safe 'blanding'" (Monotype, 2024; its 2025 report, 12 Feb 2025, reframes trends as cultural themes).

## Use carefully

Needs a reason in strategy.md [05 §1.2, §1.4]:

- 3D and gradient renders: 3D is "everywhere" (LogoLounge 2026), but keep a flat master. The evidence agrees: flat by default, depth only for tangible products on clean backgrounds [02 §K rule 9].
- Serif revival and reverse-stress type: durable as an option, but the exaggerated reverse-stress flavour will date (LogoLounge 2025); match it to real heritage.
- Rounded "Smokies" type: durable for approachable consumer brands, already saturated in direct-to-consumer and food (LogoLounge 2025).
- A mono-initial that doubles as the app icon, built from the wordmark's own DNA ("Slip Knots", LogoLounge 2026).
- Earth-tone eco palettes: becoming a category uniform (LogoLounge 2025's "sustainability bus").
- Lo-fi, photocopier and "blotch" textures: right for culture, wrong for finance or health (It's Nice That, 12 Jan 2026).
- Retro and heritage revivals, including 80s "Piano" stripes: safe with real heritage, faddish when borrowed (LogoLounge 2025 and 2026).

## Avoid

Saturated or self-dating [05 §1.2, §1.4]:

- Hexagon, sparkle and gradient "AI" marks (Fast Company, 2023; Morning Brew, 19 Jul 2026); see the next section.
- Script-L flourish monograms: "If your name starts with an 'L'... a full-on convention"; also feathers, pennants and watermelons (LogoLounge 2026).
- Stick-figure "V Ball" people and swoosh-people: "you've crossed a line... You've made a person!" (LogoLounge 2026).
- Exaggerated ink traps ("Crack") and warped "Breezy" type: "risky... unless provocation is part of the brief" (LogoLounge 2026).
- Globes, orbits and droplet swarms for anything "global" or "water" [05 §1.4].
- Generic asterisks, concentric apertures, nodes-and-edges [05 §1.4].
- Pixel and 8-bit outside gaming or retro-tech: part of the early-2000s nostalgia wave (It's Nice That, 12 Jan 2026), a synthesis rather than a cited verdict [05 §1.2].

Several of these are motifs on the audit's Any category list (script l, feather, pennant, watermelon, stick figure, v ball, generic asterisk), so the audit warns when `META.motifs` names them [code].

## The AI-era cliche

The evidence [05 §1.3]:

- Fast Company's analysis of USPTO filings found hexagons "4.6 times more likely to appear in AI logos than in logos as a whole, nearly matching the figure for crypto logos (5.4 times)" (2023).
- Morning Brew (19 Jul 2026): OpenAI's hexagonal knot "quietly set the standard for what 'serious AI' is supposed to look like. Circular. Abstract." And: "concentric logos are as associated with AI as the colors red and yellow are with fast food."
- A designer's summary of the toolkit: "the same hexagon, sparkle/star, and spiraling/rotating shapes"; LogoLounge 2026's "Faders" (monoline loops fading inside an aperture) is the same cliché refined.
- The generated-logo look: "so over-detailed it turns to mush the moment it's shrunk to a favicon" or "a bland, perfectly symmetrical swoosh that could belong to literally any company"; glossy gradients, 3D bevels, stray text, and "Inter, Roboto, purple gradients on white". The common thread: "decoration where there should be an idea" (Uncommon Blue, Jul 2026; Designlab, May 2026) [04 §2].

The rule: when the brief is AI or tech, treat hexagons, sparkles, concentric rings and apertures, orbit loops, nodes-and-edges and blue-violet gradients as category cues, famous but not unique [05 §1.3]. The audit warns on hexagon, sparkle, star, concentric, aperture, orbit, node, neural, brain, circuit, cube and pixel whenever the industry resolves to ai (cliches.md) [code].

Reach instead for [05 §1.3]:

- a proper noun, or the venture's own name worked as type;
- a mascot or character, where warmth is the strategy;
- a typographic idea: one owned quirk in a real typeface;
- a concept drawn from what the product actually does. Perplexity is the thoughtful outlier: an asterisk justified by the product's citations, built to feel "like a Scandinavian subway system... a vessel for facts" (Smith & Diction).

The same pull works on Claude: "every token I generate is a vote for the average" (b1rdmania). Diverge into structurally different directions, kill hard, never blend [04 §1.2].
