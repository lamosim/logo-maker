# How top brands and identity agencies create logos (2022–2026): process, cases, lessons, judging criteria

Research compiled 2026-10-02 from ~55 web searches/scrapes. Every factual claim below is tied to a URL in the Sources list. Where a source was paywalled (Brand New reviews), only the headline, date, agency credit and comment count are used. Where sources conflicted or were missing, this is flagged in-line rather than guessed.

## 1. PROCESS: how the top identity agencies actually work

### 1.1 The canonical phase sequence (and the artifacts each phase produces)

Across agency write-ups the sequence is remarkably stable. Helms Workshop states it as a rule: "research before decisions, strategy before design, design before rollout," and notes that "skipping straight to visuals is the most common cause of rebrand failure." Roughly a third of companies rebrand within any five-year window; most projects take 3–9 months. [S1]

**Phase 0 — Immersion / Discovery (1–6 weeks).**
Artifacts: stakeholder interviews, brand audit (how the brand is perceived inside and out), competitor/category audit, archive dig, questionnaire.
- Koto's engagement for Qonto was explicitly split into an *Immersion Phase* ("to understand Qonto and its existing brand identity") followed by a *Strategic Refinement Phase* (vision, mission, values, value proposition). [S2]
- Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv (CGH) treat discovery as problem redefinition: "We're trying to define the problem together with the client. Oftentimes the problem is not what the client thinks it is." Sagi Haviv asks every client one forcing question: *if there were one idea you could instill into a simple logo, what would it be?* [S3]
- DesignStudio's Airbnb project (2014, still the reference case for immersion) had the team stay with hosts around the world and record every aspect of their stays before designing the Bélo. [S4][S5]
- Walmart's 2025 refresh began with Walmart Creative Studio "taking a deep dive into the corporate archives" — which is where they found Sam Walton's trucker hat, the source of the new wordmark. [S6]
- Logo Geek's pro-tips list recommends open-ended brief questions ("Is there a unique story behind your business or name?", "What 5 words best describe your company?", "What is the primary message you want to convey?") rather than "what colour do you want." [S7]

**Phase 1 — Strategy / Positioning / Personality (2–8 weeks).**
Artifacts: positioning statement, brand purpose/promise, values, personality/attributes, tone-of-voice principles, a single organising idea.
- Brian Collins (COLLINS) frames the whole job as two questions: "What do we believe?" (internal) and "How do we behave?" (external) — and, in rule 96, "Who are you? Where are you going? ... The first is about authenticity; the second is about relevance." Rule 25: "Seek simplicity only on the far side of complexity. Do the work, the research... before you start crafting your solution." Rule 80: "A brand is a promise performed consistently over time." [S8]
- Koto's Bolt (checkout) identity was built on one strategic idea, "Shockingly Simple," derived from the product truth that checkout completes in 50 milliseconds. [S9]
- Mozilla's case shows strategy and expression can be decoupled across agencies and years: the "Reclaim the Internet" promise was "a strategy built with DesignStudio in 2023," and the visual system was built with JKR in 2024. [S10]
- Timelines published by mid-size studios: strategy 2–3 weeks (Snapper) to 4–8 weeks (Motto, enterprise). Motto names the main blocker: "indecision... trying to appeal to too many audiences at once." [S11][S12]

**Phase 2 — Visual territories / moodboards (overlaps with Phase 3).**
Artifacts: 2–4 "territories" (named aesthetic directions with reference imagery, type, colour, texture), each mapped back to the strategy.
- Snapper's visual-identity phase (4–6 weeks) explicitly produces "moodboards, logo concepts, typography, icon sets, and colour systems through an iterative process... Two to three rounds is typical." [S11]
- Koto on naming (same logic applies to marks): "make visual moodboards, decide upon the brand strategy, mockup press headlines and billboards — anything that starts to make the name feel 'real.'" [S13]

**Phase 3 — Concept sketching and option development.**
Artifacts: hundreds to thousands of sketches; a shortlist of vetted directions; trademark knock-out searches; in-context mock-ups.
- CGH: "Oftentimes there are thousands of sketches leading to a single design. The idea is to exhaust all possibilities." Projects run "8, 10, 12 weeks." [S3][S14]
- CGH's ordering rule: "We don't even touch colour until we've found a distinctive own-able silhouette that also feels appropriate." [S14]
- CGH will not show work-in-progress: "we will never, ever expose any sketches... Our approach is to only show designs that we can stand behind, that have gone through a rigorous testing on our end... in applications, in the context, so that... the client can literally pick any of the options, and they would work beautifully." Every option passes a preliminary trademark knock-out search before the presentation, otherwise "this whole exercise is meaningless." [S14]
- Draplin: "Always start on paper... You can get to the magic that much faster on paper, than on some cold screen." Collins rule 71: "Never create and edit at the same time... Quantity is more important than quality at the start. Mess is more." [S7][S8]
- Koto's Bolt typographic work shows how far craft goes: a bespoke typeface with Pangram Pangram, then "15 characters users would see the most: $, %, @, ", !" redrawn with the lightning motif so the brand lives in product UI, not just marketing. The team tested both the category-default blue and "lightning yellow" before choosing yellow. [S15]

**How many directions get presented, and how.**
- CGH: several options, each fully vetted and shown in real applications, presented *in person only* ("We will never present anything over a web conference"); Haviv flew to Tokyo to present to a chairman who said, "I wanted to reject your proposal, but after having seen it, I cannot." Presentations open with a warning that first impressions of an unfamiliar mark are unreliable: "the less they say, the better." The Armani Exchange mark was rejected when shown "between meetings in a rush on a white piece of paper" and approved immediately when re-shown in ads, storefronts and billboards. [S14][S16]
- Pentagram: "Every Pentagram presentation Bierut has given includes a written rationale." [S17] A fixed option count is not publicly documented; projects such as Mastercard were validated with global research in which "81 percent of consumers spontaneously recognized the new symbol without the inclusion of the Mastercard brand name." [S18]
- COLLINS rule 95 governs the first creative presentation: ban the devil's advocates, then say "New ideas can be fragile because they are unfamiliar. You may not like something you see here, but you are not allowed to say that for now... find something, anything, you like in every idea." [S8]
- Smith & Diction stage presentations theatrically when the brand warrants it (the first Gamma logo direction was shown inside a Star Wars crawl), and treat "brand moves" — "literally everything except your logo" — as the real deliverable other designers ask them about most. [S19]
- Mid-size studio norm: 2–3 rounds of feedback on the visual identity. [S11]

**Phase 4 — Refinement.**
Artifacts: optical corrections, small-size variant, colour system, lock-ups, motion tests, accessibility checks.
- Brand New's own 2024 identity (logo by Rob Clarke) documents a standard move: a separate small-use version with functional ink traps because "when the logo is reduced, the tight counterspaces do start to suffer." [S20]
- Smith & Diction's 2025 Contra work: "really small adjustments to the logo/wordmark to help for better legibility at smaller sizes." [S19]
- PayPal (Pentagram): corners "de-rounded for a sharper appearance" while the layered-letter angle stayed the same; the two blues overlap to reveal Venmo blue. [S21]

**Phase 5 — Identity system (flexible / responsive / dynamic).**
Artifacts: symbol-wordmark relationship, custom type, colour roles, motion behaviour, illustration/photography direction, sonic identity, grid/layout system, "brand moves."
- Mozilla/JKR built a system "aptly named 'Grassroots to Government'" to flex from coder tools to policy advocacy; the flag symbol is built from the M plus a displaced pixel that winks at the Shepard Fairey T-rex, and can become an ASCII mascot. [S10]
- Pentagram's MIT Media Lab identity is literally titled "How to design two dozen logos at once." [S22]
- Smith & Diction's Calendly "flex bubble" is a repeatable move: connector on the web, event start/end, partner lock-ups, breadcrumbs, photo holding shape, loading animation — "a tiny way to build up some brand equity in a move that's repeatable." [S23]
- Walmart separated the Spark from the wordmark so it can work as a standalone asset "with wider separation from the Walmart name on assets like storefronts... to reinforce the logo's status as a standalone symbol." [S6]
- Bolt (ride-hailing, in-house 2025) rebuilt the whole system for scale: Euclid replaced by Inter (990+ languages), 400+ 3D illustrations replacing "the flat Corporate Memphis style [that] had frankly grown stale," a flexible grid for multilingual legal copy, and a sonic identity in F♯7(♭9,9,11) played on a disassembled scooter. [S24]

**Phase 6 — Guidelines and governance.**
- Koto/Qonto closed with a *Brand Book Phase* consolidating the framework. [S2]
- Coca-Cola's 2026 system ships with "a new immersive Brand Center alongside tech-powered Design Intelligence tools... [to] support governance, craft, and consistency at scale" across 200+ markets. [S25]
- Haviv's rule on sequencing change: "Change your marketing, change your storefront design, change your window displays, change everything you need to change before you change the logo." [S3]

**Phase 7 — Rollout and introduction.**
- Haviv on introduction as design work: University of New Hampshire launched its mark at a hockey game, throwing thousands of shirts, mascot in the new logo, alumni present, video released next day — "Excitement is contagious if it's done right." [S3]
- Motto: internal alignment first; rollout 4–8 weeks; pitfalls are "design by committee" and "underestimating the rollout process." [S12]
- Starfish's benchmark: strategy + identity 12–20 weeks; enterprise rollouts 6–12 months. [S26]

## 2. CASE STUDIES (2021–2026)

Format for each: reason → what changed → stated rationale → reception → takeaway.

### Jaguar (Nov 2024, in-house; CCO Gerry McGovern)
- **Reason:** Hard reset to an all-electric, £100k+ luxury brand with a reduced dealer network; sales had fallen from 179,000 (2017) to 43,000 (2023). [S27][S28]
- **What changed:** New unicase/mixed-case geometric wordmark, a "Jr" monogram ("artist's mark"), the Leaper reinterpreted in negative space between horizontal lines; a teaser film with no cars, lines "Copy Nothing" and "Delete Ordinary." [S28]
- **Rationale:** McGovern: Sir William Lyons believed "A Jaguar should be a copy of nothing... New Jaguar is a brand built around Exuberant Modernism." MD Rawdon Glover: "This is a complete reset." [S28]
- **Reception:** Brand New review "All is Fair in Love and Jaguar" (Dec 4, 2024; 150 comments). Fast Company named it a top-5 branding flub of 2024, noting the rebrand became "a political wedge issue," while Auto Trader data suggested interest actually spiked. Elon Musk: "Do you sell cars?" By April 2025, ACEA data showed 49 European registrations vs 1,961 a year earlier (−97.5%) and FY24/25 global sales of 26,862 vs 180,833 in 2018 — though Jaguar had deliberately stopped producing most models during the transition. [S27][S28][S29][S30]
- **Takeaway:** The identity was judged in a vacuum because it launched a year before any product; "Copy Nothing" also severed the visual link to a 90-year heritage people still valued.

### Kia (Jan 2021, in-house)
- **Reason:** Repositioning under "Movement that inspires." **What changed:** Stylised "KIA" wordmark with a continuous line. **Rationale:** "The rhythmical, unbroken line of the logo conveys Kia's commitment to bringing moments of inspiration, while its symmetry demonstrates confidence." [S31]
- **Reception:** 26% of surveyed respondents read it as "KN"; roughly 30,000 monthly searches for "KN car" (Rerev data via Core77). Inc. called it "a total flop" for sacrificing legibility. [S32][S33]
- **Takeaway:** A wordmark must still read as the name at a glance; a rationale about rhythm cannot repair a misread.

### Burberry (Feb 2023, under new CCO Daniel Lee)
- **What changed:** Revived the 1901 Equestrian Knight Design (the winning entry of a public competition that year) and a serif wordmark, replacing the 2018 Peter Saville sans-serif created under Riccardo Tisci, which "only lasted from 2018 to 2023." **Rationale:** "The new Burberry logo is archive-inspired." [S34][S35]
- **Reception:** Widely read as the end of luxury "blanding"; the knight's return was celebrated as an equity restoration. [S35]
- **Takeaway:** Distinctive heritage assets are worth more than category conformity; five years of sans-serif sameness was reversed in one move.

### PayPal (Sept 2024, Pentagram — Andrea Trabucco-Campos)
- **What changed:** Monogram redrawn sharper (angle unchanged, corners de-rounded; overlap reveals Venmo blue); symbol no longer locked to the wordmark; custom typeface PayPal Pro; a black-and-white base palette with blues as accents, explicitly "set apart from the blue that has become synonymous with fintech"; motion built from taps, clicks, flips, swipes. [S21]
- **Reception:** Brand New reviewed it ("New Logo and Identity for PayPal by Pentagram"); designer forums called it a "yawn." [S36][S37]
- **Takeaway:** Low-drama evolution that preserved the monogram's equity while escaping a category colour; the critique was lack of ambition, not damage.

### Walmart (Jan 2025, JKR + Walmart Creative Studio; Landor on store executions)
- **What changed:** Wordmark redrawn from Sam Walton's trucker hat; custom Everyday Sans based on Antique Olive; more vibrant True Blue and Spark Yellow; the 2008 Spark elevated to a standalone asset. **Rationale:** connect heritage to a digital-first omnichannel strategy. [S6][S38]
- **Reception:** The Drum: "If it ain't broke, don't fix it." Brand New reviewed; Fast Company grouped it with 2025's most influential rebrands. [S39][S40][S41]
- **Takeaway:** Evolution, not revolution — nothing recognisable was removed; the one new ingredient came from the archive.

### Nokia (Feb 26, 2023, Lippincott)
- **Reason:** CEO Pekka Lundmark: "There was the association to smartphones and nowadays we are a business technology company." **What changed:** The 1980s blocky wordmark and deep blue replaced by abstracted letterforms with negative space; N, O, K reused as standalone graphics; "kaleidoscopic" palette. **Rationale:** "the logo's individual letters were abstracted so they only read as 'Nokia' when they act together" (purpose: "technology that helps the world act together"). [S42][S43]
- **Reception:** Broad press coverage framed it as discarding "one of the best-known mobile phone wordmarks"; nostalgia dominated public reaction. [S42]
- **Takeaway:** When the strategic goal is to *lose* an association, destroying a famous asset can be deliberate — but the replacement must earn its own distinctiveness.

### Pepsi (Mar 2023, PepsiCo Design — Mauro Porcini)
- **What changed:** First rebrand in 14 years; the wordmark returns inside the globe, flattened in the late-1980s/1990s manner; a custom typeface; "electric blue" and black (the latter borrowed from Zero Sugar). **Rationale:** "unapologetically current and undeniably Pepsi"; Zero Sugar positioned as the lead product. [S44][S45]
- **Reception:** Brand New: "New Logo, Identity, and Packaging for Pepsi done In-house"; generally positive as a reclaiming of a loved era. [S46]
- **Takeaway:** Returning to the brand's highest-fame form while adding one new, ownable colour move.

### Fanta (Apr 2023, Coca-Cola Global Design + JKR; motion by Gretel, type by Colophon)
- **What changed:** First-ever single global identity replacing regional variants; flat logo; orange roundel and leaf removed so the mark works across every flavour; light-blue shadow extended to a point. **Rationale:** Rapha Abreu wanted "playful indulgence" and to "stop any more redesigns from happening for a long time." [S47]
- **Takeaway:** Design for the whole range, not the flagship SKU; a single global mark trades local nuance for consistency.

### Reddit (Nov 2023, Pentagram — Natasha Jen)
- **Reason:** Streamline "a bit cluttered" brand ahead of a 2024 IPO and global expansion, months after the API-pricing user revolt. **What changed:** New 3D Snoo, a wordmark derived from Reddit Sans and new Reddit Display (counters and inktraps evoke speech), replacing the VAG Rounded wordmark; OrangeRed retained. **Rationale (Jen):** "we placed our trust in the Reddit team's deep understanding of their user base, as they carefully considered each of our recommended moves in relation to their communities." [S48]
- **Takeaway:** Brand maturation can be done without destroying the mascot; keep the famous colour, modernise the craft.

### Mozilla (unveiled late 2024, JKR; strategy with DesignStudio 2023)
- **What changed:** Flag symbol (M + displaced pixel, winking at the Fairey T-rex), bespoke semi-slab wordmark "in a sea of tech sans," black/white base with green, pixel-grid layout system, an ASCII mascot. **Rationale:** "Reclaim the Internet"; "people weren't getting the full picture of what we do" (Mark Surman). Built with employee/community input. [S10]
- **Takeaway:** A system for multiple audiences ("Grassroots to Government") rather than one monolithic mark.

### Verizon (June 2024, Turner Duckworth)
- **What changed:** The 2015 "electricity checkmark" replaced by a red V drawn like a folded ribbon with a yellow glow. **Rationale:** the V "anchors in the Verizon name — Veritas (truth) and Horizon (the future of possibility)." [S49][S50]
- **Reception:** Brand New reviewed ("New Logo and Identity for Verizon by Turner Duckworth"). [S50]
- **Takeaway:** A second replacement of a symbol in nine years; etymological rationales rarely survive contact with the public.

### Johnson & Johnson (Sept 2023) and Kenvue (Wolff Olins, 2022–23)
- **What changed:** J&J replaced the 1887 signature script after 136 years with a modern wordmark ("Each letter is drawn in one pen stroke"), tied to the consumer-health spin-off and renaming Janssen to J&J Innovative Medicine. The spin-off, Kenvue, got a Wolff Olins identity: a "K" where "the geometry of the rectangle represent[s] scientific precision and the round edges evok[e] the warmth of care." Wolff Olins' Daniel Renda: "Because a new name can feel unfamiliar, it's essential that identity can tell an emotional story and build meaning." [S51][S52][S53]
- **Reception:** LogoLounge headlined "Johnson & Johnson Goes Off-Script" and called the abandonment "controversial." [S52]
- **Note:** My sources do not confirm which agency drew the J&J corporate wordmark; only the Kenvue credit is verified.

### OpenAI (Feb 2025, in-house — head of design Veit Moeller)
- **What changed:** The 2016 "blossom" refined with uniform, thicker lines; custom OpenAI Sans with a perfect-circle O; a pulsing blue "emotive point"; human-curated photography. **Rationale:** "more organic and more human" while signalling research leadership. An earlier 2024 attempt was "not warmly received by employees." [S54][S55]
- **Reception:** LogoLounge: reactions "range from chilly to actively peeved, largely fueled by a sense that a technology so profound deserves something 'more'," yet judged "elegant and effective in the same way that a perfect little black dress delivers." [S55]

### Anthropic (identity by Geist; later digital expansion for Claude)
- Geist partnered with the founders "to bring them out of stealth" and "opted for a pure, typographic logo with a single standout detail," then overhauled the digital presence for Claude's launch. Refero documents an "Anthropic Serif" display face whose use in body text "signals research-publication DNA." [S56][S57]
- **Note:** I could not verify a formal 2024–25 Anthropic rebrand in the sources found; treat the Geist identity as the documented baseline.

### Cracker Barrel (Aug 2025, with consultancy Prophet)
- **Reason:** Part of a reported ~$700M transformation to attract younger, more affluent customers. **What changed:** The "Old Timer"/Uncle Herschel figure removed, leaving a text-only wordmark in a simplified barrel shape; new palette, typography, interior remodels. [S58][S59]
- **Reception:** Shares fell as much as 15% the day after the unveiling, roughly $100M of market value; President Trump and conservative activists attacked it; the company reversed within about a week ("Our new logo is going away and our 'Old Timer' will remain"), then ended its Prophet partnership. Brand New: "New Scandal and Identity for Cracker Barrel." Two of seven designers polled by Fast Company named it 2025's most influential rebrand; Design Bridge's Matt Boffey: "they eroded the brand's identity and character (literally: goodbye Uncle Herschel)." Strategic Vision's David Johnson: "they went against their brand story." [S41][S58][S59][S60][S61]
- **Takeaway:** Removing a high-fame, high-uniqueness asset is equity destruction, whatever the typography does.

### HBO Max → Max → HBO Max (2023–2025)
- 2023: renamed Max with a blue palette chosen because blue was "the most liked color, universally" (then-CMO Patrizio Spagnoletto), the dotted "a" nodding to HBO's "O." March 2025: switched to a black-and-white palette "evoking HBO." May 2025: the name reverted to HBO Max; Razorfish's case study is titled "The Rebrand That Was in on the Joke." [S62][S63]
- **Takeaway:** "Most liked" is not a brand criterion; the premium equity lived in three letters they had deleted.

### Decathlon (Mar 2024, Wolff Olins)
- From "sports retailer to global sports brand"; new icon "L'Orbit" in the retained Decathlon blue, expressing the purpose "to move people through the wonders of sport." [S64][S65]

### Eurostar (Jan 2023, DesignStudio)
- Merger of Eurostar and Thalys under "Spark New"; an animated "spark" graphic "acts as a compass symbolising the brand's purpose to spark new opportunities," used from livery to digital. [S66][S67]

### Bolt (Jan 2023, Koto — the checkout company; plus the 2025 in-house refresh of ride-hailing Bolt)
- Koto unified logo and wordmark, hiding the bolt in negative space; strategy "Shockingly Simple"; bespoke type with 15 lightning-charged UI characters; "lightning yellow" chosen over fintech blue after testing both. [S9][S15][S68]
- The unrelated mobility company Bolt refreshed in-house in 2025 because "a seven-figure agency-led refresh was out of the question" (Kaarel Kala, Head of Brand Design). [S24]

### Google "G" (2025)
- May 2025: a four-colour gradient G for Search; Sept 29, 2025: made the company-wide icon — "the brighter hues and gradient design symbolize the surge of AI-driven innovation"; applied to the Gemini spark in June. First change to the G since 2015. Designer reaction: "gradients are trending again... give it 5 years." [S69][S70]

### Twitter → X (July 2023)
- The bird and the word "tweet" were discarded; the X was "designed by a fan over the weekend." Analysts estimated $4–20B of brand value wiped out; Siegel+Gale's Steve Susi: "It took 15-plus years to earn that much equity"; Metaforce's Allen Adamson: "one of the fastest unwinding of a business and brand ever." Brand Finance's valuation fell from $5.7B (2022) toward the hundreds of millions by 2024–25. [S71][S72]

### Meta (Oct 2021, in-house)
- An infinity-loop M "designed to live in motion and 3D." LogoLounge's Bill Gardner found 1,200+ infinity-symbol logos in the database (a trend since 2008), and a near-identical 2015 mark by Maria Grønlund surfaced within days. [S73]

### 2026 rebrands worth noting
- **Coca-Cola (Jul 20, 2026):** "The logo did not change." The system instead amplifies four core assets (red/white, Dynamic Ribbon, Arden Square, Spencerian script) across 200+ markets, with a Brand Center and "Design Intelligence tools" for governance. [S25][S74]
- **KFC (Jun 2026, JKR):** A 3D logo and the "Bucketverse"; the Colonel refreshed "with a warmer expression... while staying very much like himself." [S75]
- **Threads (Jun 2026, Studio NARI):** Wordmark moved off Instagram Sans to a bespoke "Trending Chisel" letterform system; "We were interested in building a system rather than just a logo." Fast Company: "a logo worthy of its ambitions." [S76][S77]
- Also on Brand New's 2026 ledger: Sam's Club (Turner Duckworth), Great Value (JKR), Bob's Red Mill (Turner Duckworth), Intuit (JKR + in-house, reviewed as "I'm Into It"). [S78]

## 3. LESSONS: numbered rules an AI designer can follow

1. **Strategy before style; one idea per mark.** Write the one idea the logo must carry (Haviv's question) before any sketching; every direction must trace back to it (Bolt: "Shockingly Simple"; Mozilla: "Reclaim the Internet"). [S3][S9][S10]
2. **Audit distinctive assets before touching anything.** Score each existing element for fame and uniqueness (Romaniuk). Elements in the high/high quadrant (Uncle Herschel, the Twitter bird, Burberry's knight, HBO's letters) are protected; removing them is equity destruction, not modernisation. [S79][S58][S71][S35]
3. **Default to evolution; reserve revolution for a real strategic break.** Walmart, Pepsi, PayPal, Coca-Cola 2026 changed little and kept recognition; Nokia and Jaguar chose rupture because they wanted to shed an association — accept that this costs recognition and plan for it. [S6][S44][S21][S25][S42][S28]
4. **Mine the archive first.** Burberry (1901 knight), Walmart (Sam Walton's hat), Pepsi (1990s globe), Mastercard (1979 lettering), Mozilla (T-rex wink): the most-praised moves of the decade are retrievals, not inventions. [S35][S6][S44][S18][S10]
5. **Silhouette first, colour last.** Find a distinctive, ownable black-and-white shape that also feels appropriate; only then add colour (CGH). [S14]
6. **Legibility is non-negotiable; test the name read.** A wordmark that 26% of people misread (Kia → "KN") fails regardless of its rationale. Produce and check a small-size variant (ink traps, opened counters) as Brand New and Smith & Diction do. [S32][S20][S19]
7. **Escape category camouflage.** Check what colour/typeface the category defaults to (fintech blue, "a sea of tech sans," infinity loops) and deliberately differ; PayPal, Mozilla and Bolt each did this explicitly. [S21][S10][S15]
8. **Run a cliché and conflict check before presenting.** Search for shape frequency (LogoLounge found 1,200+ infinity marks when Meta launched) and run a trademark knock-out search; CGH refuses to show anything that has not cleared one. [S73][S14]
9. **Do not over-explain.** Etymological or geometric rationales (Veritas + Horizon; rectangles = precision, round edges = care; "rhythmical, unbroken line") rarely reach the public; "the less they say, the better." A simple mark "cannot say very much about your company." [S49][S52][S31][S14][S3]
10. **Simple is required but not sufficient.** "Simplicity endured" (Bierut), yet Cracker Barrel's simplified wordmark was simple *and* generic. Simplicity must coexist with a distinctive asset. [S7][S58]
11. **Never show a mark on a blank page.** Present only fully resolved options, in real applications, in person where possible; a mark approved in context was rejected on white paper (Armani Exchange). [S14][S16]
12. **Treat first reactions as noise, but equity loss as signal.** Airbnb's backlash evaporated; Cracker Barrel's did not, because the complaint was about a removed high-fame asset, not unfamiliarity. Distinguish the two before deciding whether to hold. [S3][S58]
13. **Test for recognition and distinctiveness, not likability.** Mastercard tested whether people recognised the symbol without the name (81% did). Max picked "the most liked colour universally" and reversed within two years. [S18][S62]
14. **Design the system, not the logo.** Deliver the "brand moves": type, colour roles, motion, patterns, illustration, sound, layouts. "A brand move is literally everything except your logo." Build flexible/responsive behaviour in from the start (Grassroots to Government; MIT Media Lab's two dozen logos). [S19][S10][S22]
15. **Explore wide, then converge hard.** Thousands of sketches; never create and edit at the same time; then present a few fully vetted options, each of which the client could safely pick. [S3][S8][S14]
16. **Change the logo last.** Fix marketing, product, retail, voice before the mark; a logo "should be around for a long time" and most rebrands do not need one (Gap 2010; Coca-Cola 2026 kept its logo). [S3][S25]
17. **Never launch an identity into a vacuum.** Jaguar revealed a brand a year before a product and let the internet fill the gap. Ship the identity with the thing it represents, and design the introduction (UNH hockey launch). [S28][S3]
18. **Keep the name if at all possible.** X and Max show that renaming resets 15+ years of equity and search behaviour; both faced reversal or valuation collapse. [S71][S62]
19. **Write the rationale down and tie it to the strategy, not to aesthetics.** Pentagram includes a written rationale with every presentation; judge options on "what works," "not about what one likes or dislikes." [S17][S16]
20. **Plan governance and rollout from day one.** Guidelines, a brand centre or tooling, and an internal launch sequence; underestimating rollout is the most common timeline failure. [S25][S12]

## 4. HOW CONCEPTS ARE JUDGED: the exact criteria lists and their sources

**Sagi Haviv / Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv — three criteria** (Skillshare class; multiple interviews): a logo must be (1) **appropriate** to the business "without over-explaining," (2) **distinctive and memorable** — the best logos "can be drawn from memory," and (3) **simple** — understandable "in just a few seconds." Decision principle: "choosing an effective logo is not about what one likes or dislikes: it's about what works... it must be simple." Sequence rule: distinctive ownable silhouette → appropriateness → colour last. [S80][S81][S16][S14]

**Paul Rand — seven questions** (essay "Logos, Flags, and Escutcheons," 1991): Is it **distinctive**? **visible**? **adaptable**? **memorable**? **universal**? **timeless**? **simple**? Popular scoring versions weight each 1–10 with *simple* out of 15 (max 75). [S82][S83]

**Michael Bierut / Pentagram:** "a brand new logo seldom means a thing. It is an empty vessel awaiting the meaning that will be poured into it by history and experience. The best thing a designer can do is make that vessel the right shape for what it's going to hold." Two-word rule: "Simplicity endured." Presentation rule: always a written rationale. Validation example: Mastercard's 81% recognition without the name. [S84][S7][S17][S18]

**Brian Collins / COLLINS — selected rules from "101 Design Rules":** #7 What do we believe / How do we behave (authenticity + cultural congruence); #25 simplicity on the far side of complexity; #34 "The best ideas are often expressed as simple ideas. They'll have power because they'll feel inevitable"; #59 "Design ain't what the thing looks like. Design is what the thing does"; #68 "Work with clients to help them become the only"; #71 quantity before quality at the start; #80 brand = "a promise performed consistently over time"; #95 first-presentation protocol; #96 Who are you? / Where are you going? [S8]

**Jenni Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass) — Distinctive Asset Grid** (*Building Distinctive Brand Assets*, 2018): score every asset on **Fame** (% of category buyers who link the asset to the brand) and **Uniqueness** (% of those links that go only to your brand). Quadrants: high fame/high uniqueness = use and protect; low fame/high uniqueness = "investment potential," build fame; high fame/low uniqueness = "avoid solo use," pair with a unique asset; low/low = ignore or drop. Practitioners use 50% on each axis as the working threshold. A 2026 International Journal of Advertising benchmark finds shape-based assets are the strongest class. [S79][S85][S86]

**Interbrand — ten Brand Strength factors** (Best Global Brands methodology): Leadership — Direction, Alignment, Empathy, Agility; Engagement — Distinctiveness ("uniquely ownable signature assets and experiences that are recognised and remembered by customers and difficult to replicate"), Coherence ("does it feel the same?" rather than "does it look the same?"), Participation; Relevance — Presence, Trust, Affinity. [S87]

**Aaron Draplin and Logo Geek's pro checklist:** start on paper; sketch every idea including the bad ones; keep it simple (remove anything without a purpose); avoid imitation of category colour codes; reveal the company's symbolism (write ten words, illustrate them); apply optical corrections by eye; keep old versions; deliver full-colour, black, white/inverted and single-colour versions. Draplin's visual signature is "thick lines" that survive reduction. [S7][S88][S89]

**Brand New (UnderConsideration) — the public jury:** before/after comparison in isolation and in application, a Reviewed/Noted/Spotted tier, reader polls, and year-end Best/Worst lists; headlines telegraph the verdict ("New Scandal and Identity for Cracker Barrel," "I'm Into It" for Intuit). [S58][S78]

**A practical composite scorecard (derived from the above):** 1) Appropriate to strategy/one idea; 2) Distinctive vs category (fame/uniqueness potential); 3) Simple — reproducible from memory; 4) Legible: name reads at 16 px and 1 inch; 5) Adaptable: works one-colour, inverted, in motion, as app icon; 6) Timeless: no current trend shape (gradient, infinity, ink-trap fashion) unless strategically justified; 7) Universal: no unintended readings across languages/cultures; 8) Clear of trademark conflicts and look-alikes; 9) Preserves or deliberately retires existing high-equity assets, with the trade documented; 10) Comes with a system (type, colour roles, motion, moves) and a written rationale.
