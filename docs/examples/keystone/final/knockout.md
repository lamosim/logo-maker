# Keystone: knock-out and unintended readings

The final mark: 02-arch refined (final/concept.py, stacked primary lockup, Ember on Kiln, Ember Glow and Kiln on Coal). Read and searched on 7 Oct 2026, before the first refine sheet.

These checks are a knock-out only, not legal clearance: they can surface an obvious conflict but cannot prove there is none, so have a trademark attorney run a clearance search before you file the name or spend money on it.

## Unintended readings

Read from the latest critique sheet, final/renders/round-6/critique.png, and its ladder:

- Mirrored: identical; the mark is symmetric.
- Rotated 90 degrees: a D with a funnel or megaphone on its back. Nobody sees the mark sideways; no action.
- Rotated 180 degrees (the 256 px panel turned over in the mind): a U standing on a point, close to a horseshoe magnet or a plug, the feet becoming prongs. Upright it reads as an arch; noted, no action.
- Squinting at 24 and 16 px: an arch with a raised crown. At 16 px there is a faint horseshoe-magnet or bell reading; the keystone's bump is what keeps it an arch, which is why the favicon tier keeps it 2 px proud.
- Animated: the natural motion is the keystone dropping into the crown and the arch settling. It never spins, so it cannot pass for a loading icon (the reading that set the ring direction aside).
- One colour: the 64 px and 32 px one-colour panels keep the hierarchy, the keystone the one detail.
- Colour vision: `color check` gives every role pair at 1.87:1 or more under protan, deutan, tritan and achromatopsia simulation (the weakest is Ember Glow against Kiln under deutan, dark context). The refine sheet's colour-vision strip (sheets/refine-1.png) agrees: under protan and deutan the Ember arch turns olive-brown, under tritan crimson, under achromatopsia mid-grey, and in every case it stays lighter than the near-black name, so the hierarchy holds without colour.
- Culture: an arch held by its keystone reads the same way across the West. The keystone alone is also Pennsylvania's state symbol ("the Keystone State", on its road signs) and a Masonic emblem (the Royal Arch and Mark degrees). This mark is an arch with its keystone, not a free-standing keystone shape, so neither reading leads. The brief names no cultural constraints and English only.

## What this means

- The mark: no conflicting mark found; the arch and keystone is a common idea in stock art, so `clear` stays at 3 and the drawing, not the idea, is what is ownable.
- The name: the "Keystone" app and the KEYSTONE word mark are the findings that matter most, and they are about the name, not the mark. The owner was told at the concept presentation and chose the most conservative option: keep the name and have an attorney clear it before filing or spending.
