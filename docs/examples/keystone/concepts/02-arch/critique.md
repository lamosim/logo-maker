# 02-arch: critique

Idea: a heavy round arch on its own feet whose keystone rises above the crown: the piece that holds everything together.

## Round 1

Audit ok, no failures and no warnings. 19 anchors (full and small), 16 (favicon). Thinnest feature 13.12 px at 64 px, 6.81 px at 32 px, 4.28 px at 16 px. Wordmark x-height 9.7 px in the 120 px horizontal lockup.

What the critique sheet shows:

- The lockup reads at once: arch first, then the name. The cap height (300) against an arch about 670 tall balances; the default gap of one x looks a little wide, a refinement item.
- Ladder: 256 and 128 px show the three stones and the joints; the small tier (64, 48 px) thickens the band to 200 and opens the joints to 72 units, which stay open at 32 px; the favicon tier (32 to 16 px) closes the joints into one silhouette, an arch with a raised crown.
- Mirrored: identical, as a symmetric mark should be.
- Rotated 90: a D with a wedge on its back, like a megaphone. Nobody sees the mark sideways; noted, not acted on.
- One-colour panels (64 px full symbol, 32 px small) keep the hierarchy: the keystone stays the detail.
- Upright, a thick arch on square feet can read as a horseshoe magnet. Faint; the raised keystone is what makes it an arch.

Audit warnings: none to fix or justify.

## Rationale draft

Keystone's mark is an arch that stands because of one stone. The keystone sits at the top, a little proud of the curve, and everything else leans on it. It is heavy and level, so it feels steady; it is rounded at the shoulders, so it feels warm; and it is the job Keystone does for a consultant's invoices, keeping the whole thing standing so you can get on with the work.
