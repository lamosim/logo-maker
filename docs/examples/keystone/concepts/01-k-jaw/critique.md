# 01-k-jaw: critique

Idea (round 3): Keystone's K is held together by a keystone: the stone at its junction, where the arm and the leg lean in, noses into the jaw. The folder keeps its round-1 name; the stone moved from the jaw's mouth to the junction in round 3.

## Round 1

A trapezoid stone set in the K's jaw (the counter between arm and leg), flush with the K's right edge and cut back from the letter by a 30-unit joint, so it takes the counter's shape. Audit ok; three near_miss_angles warnings (below); favicon thinnest feature 1.09 px at 16 px, barely over the 1.0 floor.

The critique sheet: the K reads, but the stone reads as an arrowhead pointing into the junction, a loudspeaker or a rewind sign, rather than a keystone; at 16 px it is a speck. Appropriate 3: below the gate. Revise.

## Round 2

A fatter stone (360 wide, 220 narrow, 165 deep) with bigger small and favicon stones. Audit ok; favicon thinnest feature 1.41 px; `near_miss_angles` now on three segments in full-symbol and small (two in the favicon): the third is the stone's own, a short straight edge at 107.9 degrees that it takes from the K's leg where `offset` cuts it back. The sheet: the stone still follows the arm's and the leg's curves into a fishtail and still points like an arrowhead. Appropriate still 3. The fault is the stone's place, not its size: a keystone sits at the crown of an arch, and the K is an arch lying on its side with its crown at the junction.

## Round 3

The stone moved to the junction: a trapezoid, 190 units against the stem and narrowing at 15 degrees, nosing 80 units past the jaw's tip, with a 30-unit joint all round, so the arm, the leg and the stem all lean on it. Small tier: joints 52, stone 210 tall. Favicon: joints 0, the stone fills the junction and noses into the jaw. Audit ok; thinnest feature 11.12 px at 64 px, 6.19 px at 32 px, 4.47 px at 16 px.

The sheet: the K reads first and the stone second, as the letter's own keystone. The rotated panel shows the construction plainly: turned 90 degrees the K is an arch with the stone at its crown. The mirrored panel is a reversed K. At 64 to 128 px the trapezoid inside the K can read as a loudspeaker: a faint reading, noted in universal. The favicon is a plain K at 16 px: the idea lives from 48 px up.

Audit warnings, justified: `near_miss_angles` in full-symbol, small and favicon, two segments each, at 104.5 and 107.9 degrees. Both are the straight edges of Bricolage Grotesque's own K leg (from (71.6, -194.1) to (111.9, -350) and from (302.8, -350) to (246.6, -176.1)): the type designer's angles, as kit-api.md says of glyph outlines used as a symbol. Redrawing the leg to 105 degrees would stop the symbol being the wordmark's K. The stone's own sides are set at exactly 15 degrees, which removed round 2's third warning.

## Scores

appropriate 4, distinctive 3, simple 4, legible 4, adaptable 4, timeless 4, universal 3, clear 4, congruent 3, system 4: 37 of 50. Evidence in scorecard.json. Congruence misses three targets (symmetry, frame, dynamism): a letter cannot be symmetric, framed and still.

Look-alike search (7 Oct 2026): generic K monograms and K-with-a-key stock logos, nothing with a keystone at the junction. These checks are a knock-out only, not legal clearance: they can surface an obvious conflict but cannot prove there is none, so have a trademark attorney run a clearance search before you file the name or spend money on it.

## Rationale draft

Keystone's K holds itself together with a keystone. The stone at the heart of the letter is what its arm and leg lean on; take it away and the K falls apart. It is a heavy, plain letter with one detail worth noticing, the way Keystone is a plain tool with one job it does better than anyone: keeping your invoices from falling through.
