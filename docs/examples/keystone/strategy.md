# Keystone: strategy

Inputs: brief.md (confirmed 7 Oct 2026); `inspiration/` is empty, so there are no reference images to take cues from; a web look at the five nearest competitors on 7 Oct 2026 (sources under section 6).

## 1. Positioning

Only statement (Neumeier): Keystone is the only invoicing tool that nags clients so you don't have to, for solo consultants in English-speaking markets who want to be paid on time without playing debt collector, in an era of stretched payment terms.

Positioning (Moore): For solo consultants who lose weeks to late payments and hate chasing clients, Keystone is an invoicing tool that follows up on every unpaid invoice by itself, steadily and politely, until it is paid. Unlike accounting suites such as FreshBooks and Wave, where reminders are one setting among hundreds, or credit-control tools such as Chaser, built for finance teams, Keystone makes the follow-up the product, sized for one person.

## 2. Archetype

- Primary, Everyman: plain, sturdy, unpretentious form; grotesque or slab type; denim blue, red, kraft; a wordmark bias. The persona is a calm site foreman: plain-spoken, practical, on your side.
- Secondary, Caregiver: rounded, enclosing, soft form; humanist or rounded type; warm neutrals, soft greens and blues; a pictorial or combination bias. Being good at follow-up is looking after someone else's money so they don't have to.
- What the pair asks of the mark: sturdy and grounded first, then rounded and enclosing; nothing slick, nothing childish. The colour rows (denim blue, soft blues and greens) collide with the owner's ban on fintech blue and the category's claimed blue and green, so the colour comes from the owner's ember and the Everyman's red and kraft instead.

## 3. Personality (Aaker, 0 to 1)

- Sincerity 0.9: "warm" and "direct" both name it (warm, honest, down-to-earth); the brand fails if chasing money feels like a debt collector.
- Excitement 0.4: the calm persona and "steady" keep it under neutral; "not boring" floors it at 0.4, so one confident detail is allowed and nothing more.
- Competence 0.9: "steady" and the persona's follow-up make reliability the job; Stripe's restraint nudges it up.
- Sophistication 0.4: a site foreman is plain-spoken, not upper-class or charming; restraint here means economy, not luxury.
- Ruggedness 0.6: the building-site persona and Patagonia's conviction lift it; "not rude" caps it, so the mark is sturdy, never aggressive.

## 4. The one idea

The piece that holds everything together.

The uniform:

- Type classes: lowercase geometric sans (Wave, Bonsai), heavy or condensed all-caps grotesque (HoneyBook, Chaser), a CamelCase sans (FreshBooks).
- Colours: blue everywhere (FreshBooks, Wave, Bonsai's Zoom blue), green as the second colour (Bonsai, FreshBooks), navy for text. The one exception is the closest competitor to Keystone's promise: Chaser, the payment chaser, owns a burnt orange.
- Motifs: a letter in a rounded tile or leaf (FreshBooks' F, Bonsai's b); slanted growth strokes (Wave); and, from the fintech list in cliches.md, upward arrows, bar charts, shields, pillars and columns, globes, handshakes, pies, dollar signs, coins and piggy banks; styles: the all-caps navy serif and bank blue.

What to avoid:

- Blue and green in any role (the owner's ban and the category's claim), and navy as the dark.
- A bare letter in a rounded tile: FreshBooks owns it, and "letter in a circle" is on the Any list.
- Slants, growth strokes and upward motion (Wave; the owner bans upward arrows).
- Columns and pillars: an arch standing on columns reads as a bank façade, which is the fintech cliché, so any arch stands on its own feet.
- Chaser's orange as Chaser uses it: a mid, coral-leaning orange in a condensed caps wordmark on white.

What is free to own:

- Construction and masonry: none of the five uses a building or a stone; the arch and its keystone are unclaimed in the category, and the name hands it over.
- A heavy, grounded, symmetric silhouette: everyone else slants, curls or sits in a tile.
- A deeper, redder ember than Chaser's (hue near 30 rather than 38, darker), deployed singularly: ember on a coal ground and on warm paper, never on white beside blue.
- A title-case wordmark with weight: the others are lowercase-friendly or shouting caps.

## 7. Feature targets

From evidence-rules.md, with the personality scores above:

- Shape 0.4, between round and angular: round for warmth and care (rule 5, sincerity 0.9), with straight, grounded structure for durability (competence 0.9, ruggedness 0.6).
- Symmetry 0.85: default to symmetry; excitement is only 0.4 (rule 6).
- Complexity low: the harshest environments are a 16 px favicon and embroidery, and competence wants simple and flat (rules 2, 19). Rule 2's warning stands: too simple reads generic, so the silhouette must be ownable, not a plain shape.
- Weight heavy: heavy strokes for competence and ruggedness (rule 10); embroidery cannot hold hairlines.
- Hue family: ember orange, a red-orange near OKLCH hue 30 (the owner's wanted colour; rule 11's red for energy, kept warm, not loud).
- Saturation medium: warm, not cute; excitement is only 0.4 (rule 11).
- Lightness mid for the ember, with a dark coal for ink (rule 11: dark for durability, light for friendliness).
- Typeface class: grotesk, sans for potency and the Everyman's plain register (rule 12); title case, as the owner writes the name.
- Frame: framed. Finance is a risky category, where a frame or badge helps (rule 8); shields are off limits, so the frame is the mark's own enclosure (an arch encloses its opening) or the app tile, never a badge or shield.
- Dynamism 0.2: stable, upright and grounded forms for trust (rule 7).
- Descriptive cue: false. This overrides rule 3 on purpose: the owner wants no tagline, and every visual cue for invoicing in this category is either on the fintech cliché list (coins, dollar signs, upward arrows) or a literal document. The wordmark leads, and the product, the site and the invoices themselves carry the description.
- Name sound: "Keystone" opens on a hard K stop and closes on a long back "o" (KEE-stone; `names analyze` gives two front and one back vowel, front ratio 0.67, a diagonal initial and no double letters). Stops read potent and competent; the back vowel reads large and strong. Pair it with a heavy, grounded mark, rounded at the shoulders, not a small, sharp, light one.

## 8. Logo type

Combination mark, wordmark-led (`combination`). Keystone is new and its name is distinctive, but at eight letters it is one longer than a wordmark can carry into an app icon, and the app icon is the first surface: so the mark needs a symbol for the icon, the favicon and the avatar, and the wordmark leads in the website header (rule 3: a new brand leads with a legible wordmark). The symbol is designed from the name's own DNA (the K, the arch, the keystone), so the two parts share construction. A mono-initial that doubles as the app icon is a "use carefully" trend (trends.md); its reason here is the eight-letter name and the icon-first ranking.

## 9. Constraints

- Minimum sizes (the brand.json defaults): horizontal lockup 120 px or 25 mm, stacked 160 px or 25 mm, mark 24 px or 6 mm, favicon 16 px; the favicon tier is redrawn, not scaled.
- One colour: the mark keeps its hierarchy in solid black and solid white, for embroidery: no hairlines, no gradients, no detail that needs two colours.
- Surfaces, ranked: app icon, website header, social avatar. The square symbol is the primary asset; the horizontal lockup is the header.
- Scripts: Latin only, English only; no cultural constraints given.
- Off limits: upward arrows and shields (the owner), and the rest of the fintech list unless twisted and justified.
- Colour: no blue or green in any role; an ember distinct from Chaser's orange.
